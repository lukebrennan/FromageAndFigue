Pairing guide

The art of pairing

What to pour, what to plate and what to put alongside. Choose a cheese and Benoit will tell you what it loves, or start with the glass in your hand.

01 · By cheese

Choose a cheese.

Pick any cheese from the counter to see the wines, dishes and little extras that suit it best. Every name links to more, and the cheese goes straight to your basket.

02 · By the glass

Start with the glass.

Already know what you are pouring? Find your wine, cider or beer below, then choose a cheese to go with it.

03 · Three rules

Benoit's three rules.

Pairing is not a science. These three habits will get you most of the way, and the rest is tasting.

  1. 01

    Match the weight

    Delicate cheeses want delicate wines, and bold cheeses can stand up to power. A light goat's cheese drowns beside a heavy red, and a twenty-four month Comté makes a thin white taste of water.

  2. 02

    Echo or contrast

    Either mirror the cheese, with nutty with nutty, or set it against something opposite. Salty blue and sweet Sauternes is the best contrast in the book.

  3. 03

    What grows together goes together

    Cider with Normandy Camembert, Vin Jaune with Jura Comté, Sancerre with Loire goat's cheese. When in doubt, drink from the same place the cheese came from.

A good pairing should make both things taste better than they do alone. If it does not, change the glass, not the cheese.

Benoit Severin-Delos

05 · Build a board

Five things, one board.

  1. One soft and creamyBrie, Camembert or Reblochon.
  2. One firm and nuttyComté or Manchego, cut thin.
  3. One bold and blueRoquefort or Gorgonzola Dolce.
  4. Something sweetHoney, a preserve, a ripe pear or quince paste.
  5. Something crunchyWalnuts, almonds, crackers and good bread.

Allow about 40 to 60 grams of each cheese per person. Take it out of the fridge an hour before serving.
